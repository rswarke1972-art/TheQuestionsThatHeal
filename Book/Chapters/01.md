# The Question After the Rain

(The rain had just stopped. Cool air drifted through the college corridors as students hurried across the campus, careful not to step into the puddles that reflected the gray evening sky. Most classrooms had already emptied, leaving behind only the faint sounds of distant conversations and footsteps.

Professor Vinay closed the psychology textbook in his hands and slipped it into his leather bag. Just as he turned toward the corridor, he noticed someone standing a few feet away.

It was Rudhika.

She clutched her notebook tightly against her chest. Her eyes followed the professor for a moment before she gathered enough courage to speak.)

**Rudhika**: Professor... may I ask you something?

(Vinay looked up and smiled. He had begun to expect this. Whenever a lecture ended, Rudhika often seemed to leave with more questions than answers.)

**Professor Vinay**: (Closing the zipper of his bag.) You usually do.

(Rudhika let out a small, nervous smile. She lowered her eyes for a brief moment, wondering if her question would sound strange.)

**Rudhika**: Why do people fear questions?

(Vinay looked at her for a moment. Instead of answering immediately, he glanced around the nearly empty corridor.)

**Professor Vinay**: Before I answer... tell me something.

**Rudhika**: (Blinking in surprise.) Me?

**Professor Vinay**: Yes. You asked the question. So what do *you* think?

(Rudhika looked down at the wet floor. Tiny ripples danced in a puddle where raindrops still fell from the roof. She remained silent for a few seconds.)

**Rudhika**: (Thinking carefully.) Maybe... because questions expose what we don't know.

**Professor Vinay**: Hmm.

**Rudhika**: (Gaining a little confidence.) If someone asks me something I can't answer... I feel embarrassed. Maybe everyone feels that way.

**Professor Vinay**: That's one possibility.

(He began walking slowly down the corridor. Rudhika followed a step behind, matching his pace.)

**Professor Vinay**: Let me ask you another question.

**Rudhika**: (Smiling faintly.) You always answer my questions with more questions.

**Professor Vinay**: (Chuckling.) Occupational hazard.

(Rudhika laughed softly.)

**Professor Vinay**: When you were a child, did you ask many questions?

**Rudhika**: Too many, according to my parents.

**Professor Vinay**: Were you afraid then?

**Rudhika**: No... not at all.

**Professor Vinay**: So something changed.

(Rudhika's smile faded as she thought about it.)

**Professor Vinay**: A four-year-old can ask, "Why is the sky blue?" without worrying about sounding foolish. An adult often knows the question... but keeps it locked inside.

**Rudhika**: Why?

**Professor Vinay**: Because somewhere along the way, many of us begin believing that asking a question is the same as admitting ignorance.

(They walked in silence for a few moments. The sound of their footsteps echoed through the corridor.)

**Professor Vinay**: But think carefully, Rudhika. Which person remains ignorant the one who asks, or the one who stays silent because they're afraid?

(Rudhika stopped walking.)

She didn't answer immediately.

Instead, she watched a group of students pass by. They laughed loudly, discussing an upcoming assignment. One of them confidently explained a topic that, from what she had heard in class, was completely wrong.

She wondered how many people spoke confidently simply because they were afraid to ask.

**Rudhika**: (Quietly.) The one who stays silent...

**Professor Vinay**: Exactly.

Knowledge rarely begins with an answer.

It almost always begins with the courage to ask a question.

(A gentle breeze drifted through the corridor, carrying the scent of rain-soaked earth. Most of the students had already left. The campus, which had been bustling only a few minutes ago, was slowly settling into silence.)

(Rudhika remained thoughtful. Vinay noticed she wasn't completely convinced.)

**Professor Vinay**: Tell me honestly.

**Rudhika**: (Looking up.) Hmm?

**Professor Vinay**: Have you ever stopped yourself from asking a question?

(Rudhika let out a quiet laugh.)

**Rudhika**: More times than I can count.

**Professor Vinay**: Why?

**Rudhika**: (She hesitated.) Sometimes... I feel everyone else already understands. If I'm the only one asking, maybe they'll think I'm slow.

**Professor Vinay**: Has anyone ever actually told you that?

**Rudhika**: No...

**Professor Vinay**: Then who told you?

(Rudhika opened her mouth to answer but stopped.)

(The question lingered in the air.)

**Rudhika**: (Softly.) I... don't know.

**Professor Vinay**: Interesting, isn't it?

(He rested one hand on the corridor railing.)

**Professor Vinay**: Sometimes the loudest critic in our lives isn't another person.

It's the voice we've built inside our own minds.

(Rudhika looked at him quietly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Psychology has a name for this.

**Rudhika**: (Her curiosity immediately returned.) It does?

**Professor Vinay**: We often create an imaginary audience.

We feel as though everyone is constantly watching us... judging every word we speak, every mistake we make.

But here's something fascinating.

Most people are too busy worrying about themselves to spend much time thinking about you.

(Rudhika blinked.)

**Rudhika**: So... everyone is busy wondering what everyone else thinks of them?

**Professor Vinay**: (Smiling.) Exactly.

Imagine twenty students sitting in a classroom.

Nineteen of them are thinking,

"I hope nobody notices I'm confused."

And the twentieth stays silent because she believes the other nineteen understand perfectly.

(Rudhika couldn't help smiling.)

**Rudhika**: That... actually sounds like my classroom.

**Professor Vinay**: More classrooms than you might imagine.

(They resumed walking toward the college garden. Rainwater still clung to the leaves, sparkling in the fading evening light.)

**Professor Vinay**: Let me ask you one last question before we go home.

**Rudhika**: (Grinning slightly.) Another one?

**Professor Vinay**: Naturally.

If a question exists only inside your mind...

and you never ask it...

who loses more?

The teacher...

or you?

(Rudhika didn't answer immediately.)

She imagined every question she had swallowed over the years.

Questions she thought were childish.

Questions she feared would make others laugh.

Questions that had quietly disappeared before they were ever spoken.

She looked at Professor Vinay.

**Rudhika**: I do.

**Professor Vinay**: (Nodding gently.) Exactly.

A teacher may lose one opportunity to explain.

But a student can lose years of understanding.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

The silence no longer felt awkward.

It felt... thoughtful.

(The evening sun briefly peeked through the clouds, casting a soft golden light across the rain-washed campus. The puddles that had mirrored gray skies now reflected streaks of orange.)

(Rudhika and Professor Vinay continued walking toward the college garden. The silence between them was comfortable.)

**Rudhika**: Professor...

**Professor Vinay**: Hmm?

**Rudhika**: Were you always like this?

**Professor Vinay**: Like what?

**Rudhika**: Confident. Calm. The kind of person who never seems afraid to speak.

(Vinay smiled quietly, then looked ahead instead of at her.)

**Professor Vinay**: Not even close.

(Rudhika looked surprised.)

**Rudhika**: Really?

**Professor Vinay**: When I was in my first year of college, I was sitting exactly where you usually sit.

The third bench.

Near the window.

(Rudhika listened intently.)

**Professor Vinay**: One day, my professor was teaching memory.

He said something that didn't make sense to me.

I thought about raising my hand...

I even lifted it halfway.

(He paused.)

**Professor Vinay**: Then I looked around.

Everyone else was writing notes.

No one seemed confused.

So I told myself, "The problem must be me."

I lowered my hand.

**Rudhika**: (Quietly.) What happened then?

**Professor Vinay**: Nothing.

That's the problem.

Nothing happened.

The class ended.

Life moved on.

But my question stayed with me.

For weeks.

(Rudhika frowned.)

**Rudhika**: You never asked it?

**Professor Vinay**: No.

I convinced myself it wasn't important.

Months later, while reading a psychology book, I found the answer by accident.

Do you know what shocked me?

(Rudhika shook her head.)

**Professor Vinay**: My confusion had been completely reasonable.

The professor had skipped an important explanation.

Several students had misunderstood the topic.

Nobody asked.

Everybody assumed everyone else understood.

(Rudhika lowered her eyes.)

**Rudhika**: So... everyone was pretending.

**Professor Vinay**: Not pretending.

Protecting their image.

There's a difference.

People often protect how intelligent they appear...

even if it costs them becoming more intelligent.

(The words settled heavily in Rudhika's mind.)

**Rudhika**: (After a long pause.) Professor...

**Professor Vinay**: Yes?

**Rudhika**: Have you ever been asked a question you couldn't answer?

(Vinay laughed.)

Not a small laugh.

A genuine, wholehearted laugh.

A few nearby pigeons fluttered into the air.

**Professor Vinay**: Almost every year.

(Rudhika stared at him.)

**Rudhika**: Seriously?

**Professor Vinay**: Of course.

Do you know what I tell my students?

**Rudhika**: What?

**Professor Vinay**: "I don't know."

(Rudhika blinked in disbelief.)

**Professor Vinay**: Then I add...

"Let's find out together."

Knowledge isn't damaged by admitting ignorance.

It's strengthened by the willingness to explore it.

(Rudhika smiled.

For the first time since the conversation began, she felt that asking questions wasn't a test she had to pass.

It was an invitation to think.)

Just then, the college bell rang in the distance.

The campus was officially closing for the day.

(The bell echoed through the campus one last time. A security guard in the distance began locking the classrooms.)

**Professor Vinay**: I suppose that's our signal to leave.

(Rudhika nodded and adjusted the strap of her bag. They walked toward the college gate at an unhurried pace.)

After a few moments, Vinay spoke again.

**Professor Vinay**: Rudhika.

**Rudhika**: Yes, Professor?

**Professor Vinay**: Can I tell you something I've noticed about you?

(Rudhika looked slightly nervous.)

**Rudhika**: Is... is something wrong?

**Professor Vinay**: Quite the opposite.

(He smiled.)

**Professor Vinay**: In every class, there are students who ask questions because they want to impress others.

There are students who ask questions because they're preparing for an examination.

And then...

there are a very few students who ask questions because they genuinely want to understand.

(He looked at her.)

**Professor Vinay**: You're one of them.

(Rudhika froze for a moment.)

She wasn't expecting that.

A faint warmth spread across her cheeks.

She quickly looked away, pretending to straighten the notebook she was already holding perfectly.

**Rudhika**: (Embarrassed.) I... I just ask whatever comes to my mind.

**Professor Vinay**: Exactly.

Never lose that.

Facts can be memorized.

Curiosity cannot.

(It was one of the simplest compliments Rudhika had ever received.

Yet somehow...

it felt like one of the most meaningful.)

(They reached the college gate. Outside, the roads still glistened from the rain.)

**Rudhika**: Professor...

**Professor Vinay**: Hmm?

**Rudhika**: Can I ask... one last question?

(Vinay laughed softly.)

**Professor Vinay**: I was wondering how long it would take.

(Rudhika smiled.)

**Rudhika**: If asking questions is so important...

then why do some people become angry when their own beliefs are questioned?

(Vinay's smile remained, but this time there was a thoughtful look in his eyes.)

He glanced at the darkening sky and then at his watch.

**Professor Vinay**: That's not a five-minute conversation.

(Rudhika looked slightly disappointed.)

**Professor Vinay**: Tomorrow.

Same time.

After class.

We'll talk about why people defend beliefs as if they're defending themselves.

(Rudhika nodded eagerly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Until then...

I want you to observe something.

Don't argue with anyone.

Don't try to change anyone's mind.

Just notice how people react when someone politely questions an opinion.

You might discover more than any textbook could teach you.

(Rudhika smiled.)

**Rudhika**: Homework?

**Professor Vinay**: (Grinning.) Psychology homework.

(The professor walked toward the faculty parking area.

Rudhika stood at the gate for a few moments, watching him disappear into the evening.

Then she looked up at the clearing sky.

Tomorrow suddenly felt much closer than it had a minute ago.)

**End of Chapter 1**
