# The Smile Hidden Behind Silence

(Monday morning.

The Psychology Department was unusually peaceful.

Soft sunlight entered through the tall windows, painting long golden rectangles across the polished floor.

Professor Vinay's office door remained half open, as it always did.

Inside...

books lined every wall.

Psychology.

Neuroscience.

Philosophy.

Sanskrit literature.

History.

Chess.

Plants rested beside the window.

A small wooden chessboard lay on one corner of the table, already arranged as if waiting for someone.

Professor Vinay stood near the bookshelf, arranging a few papers.

A gentle knock interrupted the silence.)

**Professor Vinay**: Come in.

(The door opened slowly.

Rudhika stepped inside.

She smiled...

but only for a moment.

Standing half a step behind her...

was a young woman.

She looked to be around twenty-four.

Simple blue kurta.

Hair tied carelessly.

Dark circles beneath tired eyes.

She wasn't looking at the room.

She wasn't looking at Professor Vinay.

She seemed to be looking...

through everything.

Her hands remained tightly clasped together.

Almost as if they were holding each other together.)

Rudhika spoke softly.

**Rudhika**: Good morning, Professor.

Vinay smiled warmly.

**Professor Vinay**: Good morning, Rudhika.

(His eyes gently shifted toward the young woman beside her.)

**Professor Vinay**: And you must be...

(Rudhika looked toward her sister.)

**Rudhika**: Professor...

this is my elder sister.

Adhrika.

(The young woman gave a tiny nod.)

**Adhrika**: Hello...

Barely louder than a whisper.

(Vinay smiled exactly as he smiled at every student.

No surprise.

No pity.

No curiosity.

Simply kindness.)

**Professor Vinay**: Welcome, Adhrika.

I'm glad you came.

(There was no reply.

Only another small nod.)

(The room became quiet.)

Rudhika looked from her sister...

to Professor Vinay.

**Rudhika**: Professor...

if it's alright...

could I wait outside?

I think...

she might feel more comfortable.

(Vinay nodded.)

**Professor Vinay**: That's perfectly fine.

(Rudhika turned toward her sister.)

She gently squeezed Adhrika's hand.

**Rudhika**: I'll be right outside.

Take your time.

(Adhrika nodded once.)

The door closed softly behind Rudhika.

Silence.

Not an awkward silence.

Just...

a room occupied by two strangers.

One waiting patiently.

The other wondering whether she had made a mistake by coming.

(Vinay didn't sit immediately.

Neither did Adhrika.)

After a few moments...

he spoke.

**Professor Vinay**: Tea?

Coffee?

Water?

Juice?

(Adhrika looked slightly surprised by the question.)

She answered quietly.

**Adhrika**: Water...

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Good choice.

(He poured a glass of water and placed it gently on the table.

Not directly in front of her.

Just close enough that she could take it if she wished.)

She quietly picked it up.

Took one sip.

Placed it back.

Another silence.

Vinay finally sat down.

Instead of sitting behind his desk...

he chose the chair beside it.

Leaving the chair opposite him completely free.

An invitation.

Not an interrogation.

After a few moments...

Adhrika slowly sat down as well.

Her eyes wandered across the room.

Books.

Plants.

Chessboard.

Certificates.

Photographs.

Anything except the person sitting beside her.

(Vinay noticed.

He smiled gently.)

**Professor Vinay**: Those books look intimidating.

Don't they?

(For the first time...

the corner of Adhrika's lips moved ever so slightly.)

**Adhrika**: A little.

Vinay laughed softly.

**Professor Vinay**: Don't worry.

I've read only some of them.

(That earned the smallest hint of amusement.)

It disappeared almost immediately.

But Vinay noticed it.

He didn't mention it.

He simply continued.

**Professor Vinay**: Rudhika has told me a little about you.

Only a little.

Mostly that she admires you.

(Adhrika looked down.)

**Adhrika**: She shouldn't.

Vinay didn't argue.

He simply asked.

**Professor Vinay**: Why?

Adhrika shrugged.

**Adhrika**: Nothing special.

Vinay nodded.

Another silence.

He wasn't trying to fill every empty moment.

People carrying heavy sadness often need time...

before words feel safe.

Several minutes passed.

Finally...

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: May I ask you something unusual?

(Adhrika looked up briefly.)

**Adhrika**: Hmm?

**Professor Vinay**: Let's make a deal.

No difficult questions for the next five minutes.

Only very easy ones.

You may answer with only one word if you like.

No explanations required.

If you don't want to answer...

just say "pass."

Fair?

(Adhrika looked puzzled.)

Then...

for the first time...

she gave something close to a genuine expression.

A tiny nod.

Vinay clapped his hands together once.

Very softly.

**Professor Vinay**: Excellent.

Welcome...

to the world's easiest game.

(Adhrika blinked.)

**Professor Vinay**: Ready?

She nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Tea...

or coffee?

**Adhrika**: Tea.

**Professor Vinay**: Summer...

or winter?

**Adhrika**: Winter.

**Professor Vinay**: Mountains...

or beaches?

**Adhrika**: Mountains.

**Professor Vinay**: Books...

or movies?

She hesitated.

**Adhrika**: Books.

Vinay smiled dramatically.

**Professor Vinay**: Ah...

we can still be friends.

(For the first time...

a faint smile appeared.

Small.

Fragile.

But unmistakably real.)

Vinay pretended to sigh in relief.

**Professor Vinay**: Good.

I was worried I'd have to end today's meeting early.

(The smile lasted slightly longer this time.)

Vinay continued.

**Professor Vinay**: Chocolate...

or vanilla?

**Adhrika**: Chocolate.

**Professor Vinay**: Sunrise...

or sunset?

Adhrika looked toward the window.

She thought for several seconds.

**Adhrika**: Sunset.

Vinay nodded thoughtfully.

**Professor Vinay**: Interesting.

Cats...

or dogs?

Adhrika answered immediately.

**Adhrika**: Dogs.

**Professor Vinay**: Fiction...

or biographies?

**Adhrika**: Fiction.

**Professor Vinay**: Rain...

or snow?

**Adhrika**: Rain.

**Professor Vinay**: Writing...

or reading?

**Adhrika**: Reading.

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Introvert...

or extrovert?

For the first time...

she didn't answer immediately.

Instead...

she quietly asked.

**Adhrika**: Can I say...

"Depends"?

(Vinay's eyes lit up.)

**Professor Vinay**: Absolutely.

Best answer so far.

(Without realizing it...

Adhrika smiled.

A real one.

Tiny.

But undeniably real.

She herself didn't notice it.

Professor Vinay did.

He quietly stored that moment away.

Not as a victory.

But as hope.)

**Professor Vinay**: Good.

Now let's make it a little harder.

(Adhrika raised an eyebrow.)

**Professor Vinay**: One sentence answers are allowed now.

Ready?

She gave another small nod.

**Professor Vinay**: What's your favorite season...

and why?

Adhrika thought for a few seconds.

**Adhrika**: Rain...

because everything becomes quieter.

(Vinay nodded.)

**Professor Vinay**: That's a beautiful answer.

He continued.

**Professor Vinay**: If you could spend one day anywhere...

where would you go?

**Adhrika**: Somewhere...

without people.

Vinay smiled gently.

**Professor Vinay**: Mountains?

She nodded.

**Adhrika**: Yes.

Mountains.

**Professor Vinay**: Good choice.

They're wonderful listeners.

(For the first time...

she let out a tiny laugh.)

It escaped before she could stop it.

She immediately looked away...

almost embarrassed that she had laughed.

Professor Vinay pretended not to notice.

He simply continued asking.

**Professor Vinay**: Morning person...

or night owl?

**Adhrika**: Night.

**Professor Vinay**: Why?

Adhrika looked toward the window.

**Adhrika**: Nights don't expect anything from you.

(Vinay quietly absorbed the sentence.)

He didn't analyze it.

He simply nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Fair enough.

Another question.

Suppose you could instantly master one skill.

Anything.

What would you choose?

Adhrika looked genuinely thoughtful this time.

Several seconds passed.

**Adhrika**: Painting...

I think.

**Professor Vinay**: "I think?"

Adhrika smiled faintly.

**Adhrika**: Or piano.

Maybe both.

Vinay laughed.

**Professor Vinay**: Now we're negotiating with imaginary superpowers.

I like it.

(Her smile stayed a little longer.)

**Professor Vinay**: Chocolate ice cream...

or mango?

**Adhrika**: Mango.

Vinay placed a hand dramatically over his heart.

**Professor Vinay**: I have to admit...

I'm disappointed.

She looked confused.

Vinay whispered conspiratorially.

**Professor Vinay**: Chocolate supporters are becoming an endangered species.

(This time...

she laughed properly.

Only for a second.

But it was unmistakably laughter.)

The room felt lighter.

Even the silence had changed.

It was no longer heavy.

It was comfortable.

(Vinay glanced toward the bookshelf.)

**Professor Vinay**: Do you read?

Adhrika nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Fiction?

History?

Psychology?

Mystery?

**Adhrika**: Mostly novels.

**Professor Vinay**: Favorite one?

She thought.

**Adhrika**: The Kite Runner.

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: A book that leaves many readers quietly staring at the last page.

Adhrika looked surprised.

**Adhrika**: You've read it?

Vinay pretended to look offended.

**Professor Vinay**: I do occasionally read books from my own office.

(Another laugh.)

This one came easier.

Without hesitation.

For a brief moment...

the exhausted expression disappeared from her face.

(Vinay looked toward the wooden chessboard resting on the corner of the table.)

**Professor Vinay**: One last question.

Adhrika followed his gaze.

Her eyes settled on the chessboard.

**Professor Vinay**: Do you play?

She shook her head.

**Adhrika**: Not really.

**Professor Vinay**: "Not really" usually means...

"Yes, but I'm pretending not to."

She smiled shyly.

**Adhrika**: My father taught me...

when I was little.

I forgot most of it.

**Professor Vinay**: Then we're equally qualified.

She looked at him suspiciously.

**Adhrika**: You're a psychology professor.

Vinay nodded seriously.

**Professor Vinay**: Correct.

Not a chess professor.

She couldn't help smiling again.

Vinay slowly reached for the board.

He carefully placed it between them.

**Professor Vinay**: No pressure.

Five minutes.

If you win...

you earn bragging rights forever.

If I win...

I still don't get anything.

Seems unfair.

Adhrika looked at the board.

Then at Professor Vinay.

Then back at the board.

For nearly half a minute...

she said nothing.

Finally...

she sighed softly.

**Adhrika**: Fine...

Only one game.

Vinay grinned.

**Professor Vinay**: Excellent.

Today's first real victory.

He began arranging the pieces.

Outside the office...

Rudhika sat on the wooden bench in the corridor.

She couldn't hear their conversation.

But through the small glass window in the door...

she caught a glimpse of something she hadn't seen in months.

Her sister...

was smiling.

(Vinay finished arranging the pieces.)

He gently rotated the board.

**Professor Vinay**: White or black?

Adhrika looked at the pieces.

**Adhrika**: White.

**Professor Vinay**: Ladies first.

She nodded quietly.

For a few moments...

neither of them spoke.

Only the soft sound of wooden pieces touching the board filled the room.

Adhrika opened with the king's pawn.

Vinay responded immediately.

A few moves later...

she developed her bishop.

Vinay casually moved a knight.

Adhrika frowned slightly.

She looked at the board...

then at him...

then back at the board.

She captured the knight.

Vinay scratched his head dramatically.

**Professor Vinay**: Ah...

I was rather attached to that knight.

She looked at him.

**Adhrika**: Then why did you leave it there?

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Sometimes knights like going on adventures.

She shook her head.

**Adhrika**: That's not how chess works.

Vinay raised both hands.

**Professor Vinay**: Fair point.

She continued playing.

Five minutes later...

Vinay's queen disappeared from the board.

He stared at the empty square.

**Professor Vinay**: I had a queen?

Adhrika blinked.

Then...

without warning...

she laughed.

A genuine laugh.

Not loud.

Not long.

But real.

Outside...

Rudhika looked through the small glass window.

She couldn't hear what had happened.

She only saw her sister covering her mouth...

trying to stop herself from laughing.

Rudhika's eyes slowly widened.

She lowered her head.

A smile spread across her own face.

Quietly...

she whispered to herself.

**Rudhika**: Thank you...

Professor.

Inside the office...

the game continued.

Vinay leaned back.

**Professor Vinay**: I have a feeling...

I'm in serious trouble.

Adhrika looked at the board.

**Adhrika**: You already are.

Vinay placed a hand on his chest.

**Professor Vinay**: Such confidence.

I'm proud.

A few moves later...

his king had nowhere left to escape.

Adhrika moved her queen.

**Adhrika**: Checkmate.

(Vinay stared at the board for several seconds.)

Then he nodded solemnly.

**Professor Vinay**: An honorable defeat.

Congratulations.

She smiled shyly.

**Adhrika**: Thank you.

Vinay began arranging the pieces again.

**Professor Vinay**: One more?

She hesitated.

Only for a moment.

**Adhrika**: Okay.

But this time...

don't lose so quickly.

Vinay widened his eyes innocently.

**Professor Vinay**: Excuse me?

I'm giving my best.

She folded her arms.

**Adhrika**: No.

You're not.

Vinay tilted his head.

**Professor Vinay**: Oh?

Adhrika pointed toward the board.

**Adhrika**: You saw my queen hanging twice.

You ignored it.

Then you sacrificed your rook for no reason.

And...

you walked straight into checkmate.

Nobody does that accidentally.

(Vinay couldn't hold back a soft laugh.)

He rubbed the back of his neck.

**Professor Vinay**: I suppose...

I wasn't as subtle as I imagined.

Adhrika pouted slightly.

**Adhrika**: You're letting me win.

Vinay looked at her for a quiet moment.

Then he smiled warmly.

**Professor Vinay**: My purpose wasn't to win...

it was to see you smile.

(The room became still.)

Adhrika's expression softened.

She looked away almost immediately.

Trying very hard to hide the smile that had returned to her face.

**Adhrika**: That's cheating.

Vinay chuckled.

**Professor Vinay**: Is it?

Adhrika nodded.

**Adhrika**: A little.

Vinay laughed.

**Professor Vinay**: Then I'm guilty.

This time...

I'll play properly.

No mercy.

Adhrika looked back at the board.

The old spark that had been hidden beneath months of silence flickered for just an instant.

She straightened in her chair.

A tiny smile appeared.

**Adhrika**: Good.

Because I was going to beat you anyway.

Vinay grinned.

**Professor Vinay**: Now that's the confidence I was waiting for.

He reached for the king's pawn.

**Professor Vinay**: Your move.

(The second game began.

Unlike before...

Professor Vinay no longer made obviously careless moves.

He developed his pieces calmly.

Adhrika watched the board with surprising concentration.

Her fingers hovered over a knight.

She hesitated.

Then moved it.

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Nice.

Controlling the center already.

Adhrika looked up.

**Adhrika**: My father always used to say...

"If you don't fight for the center...

the game slowly slips away."

(The words escaped her mouth before she realized what she had said.)

Her hand stopped moving.

The smile faded.

For a brief moment...

the room became quiet again.

Vinay didn't interrupt the silence.

He simply made his move.

Several more moves passed.

Adhrika slowly relaxed again.

She attacked his bishop.

Vinay retreated.

She smiled proudly.

**Adhrika**: See?

Vinay nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Nicely done.

A few moves later...

Vinay leaned closer to the board.

**Professor Vinay**: Hmm...

I seem to have created a problem for myself.

Adhrika looked carefully.

She studied every square.

Her eyes moved between the pieces.

Nearly a minute passed.

Then...

she looked up.

**Adhrika**: You're trying to distract me.

Vinay blinked innocently.

**Professor Vinay**: Me?

Never.

She pointed at the board.

**Adhrika**: If I take your bishop...

your queen comes here.

Then...

check.

Vinay looked at the position.

He slowly smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Very good.

You saw it.

She shrugged.

**Adhrika**: It wasn't that difficult.

Vinay laughed.

**Professor Vinay**: It wasn't...

because you found it.

Outside...

Rudhika shifted slightly on the bench.

She looked at the closed office door.

The conversation had been going on for almost an hour.

She expected her sister to walk out by now.

Instead...

she heard something.

A laugh.

Soft...

but unmistakable.

She closed her eyes.

A tear rolled gently down her cheek.

Not from sadness.

From relief.

She quickly wiped it away before anyone passing through the corridor could notice.

Inside...

the game entered its final stage.

Only a few pieces remained.

Adhrika's brow furrowed.

Vinay rested his chin on one hand.

Neither of them spoke.

Finally...

Adhrika moved her rook.

Vinay looked at the board.

Then at her.

Then back at the board.

He smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Checkmate.

She looked uncertain.

**Adhrika**: Really?

Vinay nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Unless I've secretly invented a new version of chess...

yes.

She stared at the board for another few seconds.

Then...

a smile slowly spread across her face.

This time...

she didn't try to hide it.

She leaned back in her chair.

**Adhrika**: I actually won.

Vinay folded his hands dramatically.

**Professor Vinay**: Twice.

Against a university professor.

I suppose my reputation is ruined.

She giggled.

**Adhrika**: You're exaggerating.

**Professor Vinay**: I prefer the word "dramatic."

She shook her head.

**Adhrika**: Definitely dramatic.

Vinay placed the last captured piece back into the box.

Then he looked at her.

Not as a professor examining a student.

Not as someone searching for answers.

Just as one human being looking at another.

**Professor Vinay**: May I tell you something?

Adhrika nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: When you first walked into this room...

you hardly looked at me.

You answered with nods.

Mostly one-word replies.

Now...

you've defeated me twice...

accused me of cheating...

called me dramatic...

and laughed more than once.

(Adhrika lowered her eyes.

A faint blush appeared across her cheeks.)

Vinay smiled gently.

**Professor Vinay**: I think...

that's a much better beginning than either of us expected.

For a few moments...

she simply looked at the chessboard.

Her fingers lightly touched one of the pawns.

She took a slow breath.

Then another.

Finally...

she spoke.

Her voice was quiet.

But no longer distant.

**Adhrika**: Professor...

can I ask you something?

**Professor Vinay**: Of course.

Adhrika looked out of the window.

The smile that had returned so briefly faded into something deeper.

Something heavier.

**Adhrika**: Do you think...

someone can forget...

a person they truly loved?

(Vinay looked at her for a few moments.

He didn't answer immediately.

Instead...

he slowly closed the chessboard.

One by one...

he placed every piece back into its compartment.

The king.

The queen.

The bishops.

The knights.

The rooks.

Finally...

the pawns.

He closed the lid gently.)

**Professor Vinay**: That's a very important question.

And I don't think it deserves a rushed answer.

(Adhrika nodded quietly.)

Vinay stood up.

He walked toward the small shelf near the window and poured two glasses of water.

He handed one to her.

She accepted it with both hands.

Neither of them spoke.

The silence no longer felt uncomfortable.

It felt...

safe.

Vinay returned to his chair.

**Professor Vinay**: Before I answer...

may I ask you something?

**Adhrika**: Hmm.

**Professor Vinay**: Is this question about someone...

or about yourself?

(She looked down at the glass in her hands.)

For several seconds...

nothing moved except her thumb tracing its rim.

Finally...

she gave the smallest nod.

**Adhrika**: Myself.

Vinay nodded gently.

**Professor Vinay**: I thought so.

Another quiet silence filled the room.

Outside...

Rudhika remained seated on the wooden bench.

She had stopped worrying.

Instead...

she found herself wondering what they were talking about.

Whatever it was...

it had already given her sister something she hadn't seen in a very long time.

A reason to smile.

Inside...

Adhrika took a slow breath.

Then another.

She looked at Professor Vinay.

For the first time since entering the room...

she met his eyes without quickly looking away.

**Adhrika**: Professor...

I don't think I've ever told this story to anyone.

Not completely.

Not even Rudhika.

(Vinay remained silent.

He didn't reach for a notebook.

He didn't interrupt.

He simply listened.)

Adhrika lowered her gaze again.

Her fingers tightened around the glass.

**Adhrika**: I don't even know where to begin...

Vinay smiled softly.

**Professor Vinay**: Then don't begin where it hurts the most.

Begin wherever your heart finds it easiest.

There is no right order for telling a story.

Only your order.

(Adhrika closed her eyes for a brief moment.

When she opened them again...

they carried the weight of memories she had been holding alone for years.)

**Adhrika**: Then...

I guess...

it started the day I met him.

(For a long time...

Adhrika simply looked at the floor.

As though the memories were scattered there.

Waiting to be picked up...

one by one.)

**Adhrika**: It was...

about four years ago.

I had just started college.

I wasn't looking for a relationship.

Honestly...

I never even thought about it.

I was happy with my little world.

College.

Home.

Books.

Rudhika.

That was enough.

(She smiled faintly.)

**Adhrika**: Then...

one day...

he sat beside me in the library.

There were dozens of empty seats.

But...

he chose that one.

I remember thinking...

"What a strange person."

(Vinay smiled quietly but said nothing.)

Adhrika continued.

**Adhrika**: We didn't even speak that day.

The next day...

he was there again.

Then again.

Then...

one afternoon...

he asked if he could borrow my notes.

I almost said no.

But...

I gave them to him.

(She let out a soft, nostalgic laugh.)

**Adhrika**: He returned them the very next day.

Every page...

covered neatly.

Not a single fold.

Not a single stain.

He even thanked me with a small handwritten note.

No one had ever done something like that before.

I don't know why...

but I kept that little piece of paper.

For years.

(She paused.)

**Adhrika**: We became friends.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

He was funny.

Not the loud kind.

The quiet kind.

He knew exactly how to make me laugh...

without trying too hard.

He remembered small things.

Which author I liked.

My favorite tea.

The songs I skipped.

The flowers I always stopped to look at while walking home.

Sometimes...

he remembered those things better than I did.

(Her eyes became distant.)

**Adhrika**: Nobody had ever paid that much attention to me.

Not like that.

I think...

that's when I started falling in love.

(She smiled.

Then...

almost immediately...

the smile disappeared.)

**Adhrika**: At least...

I thought it was love.

(Professor Vinay remained completely still.

His full attention rested on her words.)

Adhrika took a slow breath.

**Adhrika**: We spent almost every day together.

Studied together.

Walked together.

Celebrated birthdays.

Shared dreams.

We even talked about the future.

Not every day...

but often enough that it felt...

real.

(She swallowed.)

**Adhrika**: I trusted him.

Completely.

More than I had trusted anyone outside my family.

Maybe...

even more than I trusted myself.

(Her fingers tightened around the glass.)

**Adhrika**: When people asked...

"How are you two?"

I would smile without thinking.

Because somewhere inside...

I had already begun imagining a life with him.

A home.

A family.

Growing old together.

(Silence filled the room.)

She stared through the window.

The sunlight that had seemed warm only moments ago now felt strangely distant.

Adhrika's voice became quieter.

Almost fragile.

**Adhrika**: Then...

little things began changing.

At first...

I told myself I was imagining them.

He became busy.

Replies became shorter.

Calls became fewer.

Plans were cancelled.

Smiles became polite instead of genuine.

I kept making excuses for him.

"He's stressed."

"He's tired."

"He'll be fine after exams."

"I shouldn't overthink."

(She shook her head slowly.)

**Adhrika**: It's strange...

how easily we become lawyers...

defending the people we love.

Even when they never asked us to.

(Her eyes began to glisten.)

**Adhrika**: I didn't notice...

or maybe I didn't want to notice...

that I was the only one still holding on.

She stopped speaking.

The room fell silent once again.

Professor Vinay didn't rush to fill it.

He simply sat there...

listening.

Waiting.

Giving her heart the time it needed before speaking again.

(Adhrika closed her eyes.

A tear escaped before she realized it.

She quickly wiped it away.)

**Adhrika**: One evening...

I asked him...

"Is everything okay?"

He smiled.

The same smile I'd fallen in love with.

Then he said...

"You're overthinking."

(She laughed softly.)

It wasn't a happy laugh.

It carried exhaustion.

**Adhrika**: So...

I believed him.

I apologized...

for asking.

(Vinay's expression remained calm.

He didn't interrupt.)

**Adhrika**: Weeks passed.

Then months.

He was still there...

but not really.

Sometimes we'd sit together...

and it felt like I was sitting beside a stranger wearing the face of someone I loved.

I kept trying harder.

I called first.

I texted first.

I planned everything.

I apologized...

even when I didn't know what I had done wrong.

(Her voice became quieter.)

**Adhrika**: Every morning...

I wondered...

"What can I do today to make him happy again?"

Every night...

I blamed myself because I couldn't.

(She looked at her trembling hands.)

**Adhrika**: It was exhausting.

But...

I thought...

that's what love is.

You don't give up.

You keep trying.

Even if you're the only one trying.

(A long silence followed.)

The ticking of the wall clock became strangely noticeable.

Adhrika stared at the chessboard.

Her fingertip gently touched one of the pawns still lying beside the closed box.

**Adhrika**: Then...

one day...

he asked if we could meet.

His voice sounded different.

Calm.

Too calm.

I knew...

something was wrong.

(Another tear rolled down her cheek.)

**Adhrika**: We met at the little café where we'd had our first cup of tea together.

I remember everything.

The smell of cardamom.

The old songs playing in the background.

Even the rain outside.

Everything...

except how to breathe.

(She smiled bitterly.)

**Adhrika**: He didn't look at me.

Not once.

He kept staring into his cup.

Then...

he said...

"I don't think this is working anymore."

(Her lips trembled.)

**Adhrika**: Just one sentence.

One sentence...

and the future I'd been building in my mind for years...

collapsed.

(She swallowed hard.)

**Adhrika**: I asked him why.

He said...

"I've changed."

I asked...

"Did I do something wrong?"

He shook his head.

I asked...

"Can we fix it?"

He said...

"I don't want to."

(Her voice broke.)

**Adhrika**: That hurt more than anything else.

Not because he wanted to leave...

but because...

he had already left long before that day.

I was the last person to realize it.

(Tears now flowed freely down her face.)

She made no attempt to stop them.

**Adhrika**: I begged him...

Professor.

I actually begged him.

I promised I'd change.

I promised I'd become better.

I asked for one more chance.

Just one.

(She lowered her head.)

**Adhrika**: Looking back...

I don't even recognize that girl.

But at that moment...

I would have done anything...

just to hear him say...

"Stay."

(Her breathing became uneven.)

She covered her face with both hands.

For several moments...

she couldn't continue.

Professor Vinay quietly slid the box of tissues across the table.

He didn't speak.

He didn't say,

"It's okay."

He didn't say,

"Be strong."

He simply remained there.

Present.

Patient.

When Adhrika had wiped her eyes...

she looked up again.

Her eyes were red.

Her voice had become almost a whisper.

**Adhrika**: He stood up.

He said...

"I'm sorry."

Then he walked away.

I kept sitting there.

I don't know for how long.

People came.

People left.

The waiter asked if I wanted anything else.

I don't even remember answering him.

I just remember looking at the empty chair across from me...

hoping...

he would come back and say...

"I made a mistake."

(She smiled sadly.)

**Adhrika**: He never did.

(Once again...

the room fell into silence.

Outside...

the corridor bustled with students changing classes.

Inside the office...

time itself seemed to slow.

Professor Vinay didn't rush to respond.

He allowed her story...

and her grief...

to settle into the quiet between them.)

(The silence remained.

Not because Professor Vinay didn't know what to say...

but because some moments deserve to be heard...

before they are answered.

A gentle breeze entered through the open window.

It stirred the leaves of the small plant resting on the sill.

Adhrika stared at the table.

Her voice had become tired.

Almost empty.)

**Adhrika**: After that...

everything reminded me of him.

Songs.

Roads.

Rain.

Tea.

Books.

Even sunsets.

I stopped listening to music.

I stopped reading novels.

I stopped going to places we used to visit.

(She gave a faint, weary smile.)

**Adhrika**: It felt like the whole world had conspired against me.

Everywhere I looked...

there was another memory waiting.

Another reason to cry.

(Vinay listened quietly.)

**Adhrika**: Rudhika tried so many times to cheer me up.

She never gave up.

But...

I couldn't tell her everything.

She's my younger sister.

She's supposed to lean on me...

not the other way around.

(Her eyes filled again.)

**Adhrika**: I watched her pretending not to worry.

Pretending not to notice that I barely ate.

Pretending not to notice that I stayed awake all night.

Sometimes...

I'd hear her crying quietly in her room.

Because of me.

(She covered her eyes.)

**Adhrika**: I became the very thing I always promised I'd never become...

another burden for her.

(The words hung in the air.)

For the first time since she had entered the room...

Professor Vinay spoke.

His voice was calm.

Almost gentle enough to become part of the silence.)

**Professor Vinay**: Adhrika...

may I ask you a question?

She nodded weakly.

**Professor Vinay**: If Rudhika were sitting where you are...

telling me the same story...

would you think she was a burden?

(Adhrika answered immediately.)

**Adhrika**: No.

**Professor Vinay**: Why not?

Adhrika looked surprised by the question.

**Adhrika**: Because...

she'd be hurting.

She'd need someone.

Vinay nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Then why does kindness apply to her...

but not to you?

(Adhrika opened her mouth...

then closed it again.)

She searched for an answer.

None came.

Vinay continued softly.

**Professor Vinay**: You've given compassion to everyone in your story.

To him.

To Rudhika.

Even to strangers.

But somewhere along the way...

you quietly removed yourself from the list of people allowed to receive it.

(Adhrika lowered her eyes.)

A tear fell onto the back of her hand.

Vinay didn't stop.

His tone remained warm.

Never accusing.

Never correcting.

Simply inviting her to think.

**Professor Vinay**: Tell me honestly...

during those months after the breakup...

what hurt you the most?

Was it loneliness?

Was it rejection?

Was it losing the future you imagined?

Or was it something else?

(Adhrika sat very still.

She thought for a long time.

Long enough that the ticking clock once again became noticeable.)

Finally...

she whispered.

**Adhrika**: I think...

it was the feeling...

that I wasn't enough.

That if I had been prettier...

or smarter...

or more interesting...

he would have stayed.

(Her voice cracked.)

**Adhrika**: Every day...

I kept asking myself...

"What was wrong with me?"

(She looked directly at Professor Vinay for the first time since telling her story.)

**Adhrika**: Professor...

was I not enough?

(Professor Vinay did not answer immediately.

Instead...

he leaned back slightly.

Not to create distance...

but to give the question the space it deserved.

He looked at Adhrika for a long moment.

Then he spoke.)

**Professor Vinay**: May I answer your question...

with a few questions of my own?

(Adhrika gave a faint nod.)

**Professor Vinay**: Suppose...

a gardener plants two roses.

He waters both.

Cares for both.

One blooms beautifully.

The other is uprooted by a passerby.

Tell me...

was the second rose less beautiful...

because someone pulled it out?

(Adhrika looked down.)

She slowly shook her head.

**Adhrika**: No.

**Professor Vinay**: Why not?

**Adhrika**: Because...

someone else's action...

doesn't decide its beauty.

(Vinay smiled gently.)

**Professor Vinay**: Exactly.

(He allowed the words to settle.)

**Professor Vinay**: Now another question.

If someone walks into a library...

looks around for five minutes...

and says,

"There are no good books here."

Does that statement suddenly become true?

**Adhrika**: No.

**Professor Vinay**: Why?

**Adhrika**: Because...

they didn't really know the books.

Vinay nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Sometimes...

people leave relationships for reasons that genuinely have nothing to do with the other person's worth.

Sometimes they change.

Sometimes they become confused.

Sometimes they make poor decisions.

Sometimes...

they simply choose a different path.

Whatever the reason...

their choice is still their choice.

It is not a measuring tape for your value.

(Adhrika listened quietly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Let me ask you one more thing.

During your relationship...

did he ever tell you...

"You are not enough"?

She immediately shook her head.

**Adhrika**: No.

**Professor Vinay**: Then...

who kept repeating those words?

(She froze.)

Her lips parted slightly.

The answer came almost as a whisper.

**Adhrika**: ...

I did.

(Vinay nodded.)

**Professor Vinay**: After he left...

his footsteps disappeared.

But another voice remained.

A much harsher one.

Your own.

(Adhrika stared silently at the table.)

**Professor Vinay**: Every morning...

who reminded you that you weren't enough?

Adhrika closed her eyes.

**Adhrika**: Me.

**Professor Vinay**: Every night...

who replayed every conversation?

**Adhrika**: Me.

**Professor Vinay**: Who searched for imaginary mistakes...

hoping to find a reason?

Her eyes slowly filled again.

**Adhrika**: Me...

(Vinay's voice remained calm.)

**Professor Vinay**: Then perhaps...

the person who has hurt you the longest...

is no longer the man who walked away.

(He paused.)

**Professor Vinay**: It's the voice that stayed behind.

(The room became silent.)

Adhrika wasn't offended.

She wasn't defensive.

She simply sat there...

thinking.

For the first time...

not about him.

But about herself.

After nearly a minute...

she spoke.

**Adhrika**: Professor...

how do I make that voice stop?

Because...

I'm tired.

So...

so tired.

(She looked utterly exhausted.)

Not exhausted from crying.

Not exhausted from missing someone.

Exhausted from fighting herself.

Professor Vinay folded his hands gently.

His expression was filled with compassion.

**Professor Vinay**: I don't think we begin by trying to silence that voice.

(Adhrika looked up.)

**Professor Vinay**: We begin...

by questioning whether it deserves to be believed.

(She frowned slightly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Just because a thought appears...

doesn't make it true.

Just because the mind repeats something a thousand times...

doesn't make it a fact.

Our minds are capable of imagination...

memory...

creativity...

and unfortunately...

self-deception too.

(He smiled softly.)

**Professor Vinay**: The mind is a wonderful servant.

But not always a reliable witness.

(Adhrika sat quietly...

absorbing every word.)

She had come into the room believing she needed an answer about the man who had left.

For the first time...

she began to wonder whether the more important question...

was about the voice that never had.

(Adhrika remained silent.

She lowered her eyes once again.

Her fingers gently traced the edge of the empty glass.

Several thoughts seemed to be passing through her mind at once.

Finally...

she spoke.)

**Adhrika**: Professor...

may I ask you something else?

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Of course.

Adhrika took a slow breath.

**Adhrika**: If...

what I'm feeling isn't because I wasn't enough...

then why does it still hurt so much?

It's been so long.

I know he's gone.

I know he isn't coming back.

I tell myself that every day.

But...

(her voice trembled)

...why can't my heart understand what my mind already knows?

(Vinay remained thoughtful.

He didn't answer immediately.

Instead...

he asked quietly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Tell me...

if someone loses a very dear pet dog...

would they cry?

Adhrika looked slightly surprised.

**Adhrika**: Of course.

**Professor Vinay**: Even if they knew...

from the very first day they adopted it...

that a dog's life is much shorter than a human's?

She nodded.

**Adhrika**: Yes.

**Professor Vinay**: Why?

Adhrika looked down.

She thought carefully.

**Adhrika**: Because...

they become attached.

Vinay nodded gently.

**Professor Vinay**: Yes.

Attachment.

(He paused.)

**Professor Vinay**: Notice something interesting.

The pain isn't caused because they were wrong to love the dog.

The pain comes because the mind keeps saying,

"It shouldn't have ended."

(Adhrika listened quietly.)

**Professor Vinay**: The heart says,

"I miss someone precious."

The attached mind says,

"I cannot accept that they're gone."

Those are not exactly the same.

(Adhrika frowned slightly.)

**Adhrika**: But...

isn't attachment part of love?

I always thought...

if you truly love someone...

you naturally become attached.

(Vinay smiled.)

**Professor Vinay**: Many people believe that.

May I ask you another question?

She nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: If I hold this glass gently...

am I holding it?

**Adhrika**: Yes.

**Professor Vinay**: If I squeeze it with all my strength...

afraid someone might take it away...

am I still holding it?

She looked at the glass in her hands.

**Adhrika**: Yes...

**Professor Vinay**: Is it the same kind of holding?

(She became thoughtful.)

After a few moments...

she slowly shook her head.

**Adhrika**: No.

One is...

gentle.

The other...

is fear.

Vinay's smile widened.

**Professor Vinay**: Exactly.

Love can hold.

Attachment clings.

Love appreciates.

Attachment possesses.

Love says,

"I'm grateful our paths crossed."

Attachment says,

"I cannot allow our paths to separate."

(The room became quiet.)

Adhrika wasn't convinced yet.

She leaned forward slightly.

**Adhrika**: But...

if there is no attachment...

wouldn't people leave each other easily?

Wouldn't love become...

cold?

(Vinay chuckled softly.)

**Professor Vinay**: That's a wonderful question.

In fact...

it's one that philosophers have discussed for centuries.

He leaned back in his chair.

**Professor Vinay**: Tell me...

have you ever seen parents sitting quietly in a park...

watching their little child learn to walk?

Adhrika nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: What do they want?

For the child to remain dependent forever?

Or...

for the child to eventually walk confidently on their own?

Adhrika answered almost immediately.

**Adhrika**: To walk on their own.

**Professor Vinay**: Even though that means...

one day the child may move away...

build a different life...

and not need them every moment?

She nodded again.

**Adhrika**: Yes.

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Isn't that interesting?

Real love rejoices in another person's growth...

even when that growth creates distance.

(Adhrika lowered her gaze.)

She didn't speak.

But something in her expression had changed.

Not agreement.

Not yet.

Curiosity.

She looked back at Professor Vinay.

**Adhrika**: Then...

what exactly is love?

And...

if attachment isn't love...

how do I know which one I was feeling?

(The question lingered quietly between them.

Professor Vinay folded his hands.

A gentle smile appeared on his face.

He knew...

they had finally arrived at the conversation that mattered most.)

(Vinay remained quiet for several moments.

He looked toward the window.

Students crossed the courtyard below.

Some walked alone.

Some laughed with friends.

Some hurried toward their next class.

Life...

without asking anyone's permission...

continued moving.)

**Professor Vinay**: Before I answer...

I'd like to ask you about someone else.

Not you.

Not him.

Would that be alright?

Adhrika nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Imagine an elderly woman.

She has a small garden.

Every morning...

she waters the flowers.

Removes dry leaves.

Protects them from insects.

One day...

a traveler passes by.

He stops for a while.

Looks at the flowers.

Smiles.

The old woman notices that he especially likes one white jasmine plant.

So...

before he leaves...

she cuts a single blossom.

Places it gently in his hand.

And says,

"I hope its fragrance stays with you."

(He looked at Adhrika.)

**Professor Vinay**: Tell me...

did she love her flower?

**Adhrika**: Yes.

**Professor Vinay**: Then why did she let it go?

Adhrika thought.

**Adhrika**: Because...

sharing it didn't reduce her love.

Vinay smiled softly.

**Professor Vinay**: Good.

Now imagine a different gardener.

He builds high walls.

Locks the gate.

Lets no one enter.

He fears someone may touch his flowers.

He checks them every hour.

He becomes anxious whenever the wind is strong.

Tell me...

who is he protecting?

The flowers?

Or his fear?

(Adhrika answered after a pause.)

**Adhrika**: ...

His fear.

Vinay nodded.

**Professor Vinay**: Exactly.

Sometimes...

what we call love...

is actually fear wearing love's clothes.

Fear of being abandoned.

Fear of being replaced.

Fear of being alone.

Fear of losing the future we imagined.

(Adhrika listened intently.)

**Professor Vinay**: Love and fear can exist together.

But they are not the same thing.

Fear whispers,

"If this person leaves...

I will become incomplete."

Love whispers,

"I am grateful you exist...

whether or not life allows us to walk together forever."

(The room fell silent.)

Adhrika looked down.

**Adhrika**: I don't think...

I ever said those words.

"I'm grateful."

I mostly kept thinking...

"I hope he never leaves."

(Vinay didn't say "Exactly."

Instead...)

**Professor Vinay**: Which sentence filled more of your days?

"I enjoy today's conversation."

Or...

"I hope nothing ruins tomorrow."

(She closed her eyes.)

Memories surfaced.

Waiting for messages.

Checking her phone.

Reading old conversations.

Feeling relieved when he replied.

Feeling anxious when he didn't.

She opened her eyes again.

**Adhrika**: ...

The second one.

Almost every day.

(Vinay nodded slowly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Then perhaps...

your love slowly became surrounded by anxiety.

Not because you chose it.

Not because you were weak.

Simply because the human mind often confuses closeness...

with certainty.

And life rarely offers certainty.

(He picked up a single white pawn from the chessboard box that still sat nearby.)

He placed it gently on the table.

**Professor Vinay**: Look at this pawn.

When the game begins...

it stands beside seven others.

As the game continues...

pieces leave the board.

Some are captured.

Some survive.

Can the pawn refuse to play...

because one day it may disappear?

Adhrika smiled faintly.

**Adhrika**: No.

**Professor Vinay**: Can it demand,

"I'll only play if every piece stays forever"?

She let out the smallest laugh.

**Adhrika**: That wouldn't be chess anymore.

**Professor Vinay**: Precisely.

Life asks something similar of us.

It never promises,

"Nothing you love will change."

It only offers the chance...

to love while it is here.

(The pawn remained between them.)

**Professor Vinay**: There is an old insight found in many traditions.

It can be expressed in different words, but the idea is similar:

Suffering grows when the mind insists that what is changing...

must not change.

That doesn't mean we shouldn't grieve.

Grief is natural.

When someone dear leaves our life...

whether through distance, choice, or death...

our hearts ache.

That ache is part of loving.

But there is another layer we often add ourselves.

The argument with reality.

"If only..."

"It wasn't supposed to happen."

"I cannot accept this."

Those thoughts keep reopening the wound.

(Adhrika's eyes rested on the pawn.)

**Adhrika**: So...

missing someone...

isn't the same as refusing to let them go?

Vinay's expression brightened.

**Professor Vinay**: That's an important distinction.

Yes.

You can miss someone deeply...

and still accept that they are no longer part of your life.

Acceptance doesn't erase affection.

It simply stops fighting what is already true.

(A long silence followed.)

This time...

it wasn't heavy.

It was thoughtful.

After nearly a minute...

Adhrika spoke.

Her voice was softer than before.

**Adhrika**: Professor...

I think...

for two years...

I wasn't trying to get him back.

I was trying to get my old life back.

The one where I believed everything would be okay.

(Vinay looked at her with quiet appreciation.)

He didn't praise her.

He didn't say,

"That's the answer."

He simply asked one final question.

**Professor Vinay**: And if that old life cannot return...

does that necessarily mean...

a meaningful new one cannot begin?

(Adhrika didn't answer.

Not because she disagreed.

But because...

for the first time in a very long while...

the possibility had never occurred to her.)

(The room grew quiet once more.

Outside...

the last lecture of the day had ended.

Students walked through the corridors.

Some laughed loudly.

Some hurried toward the gates.

Some complained about assignments.

Life...

continued with its ordinary rhythm.

Inside the office...

three people sat in extraordinary silence.

Not because there was nothing left to say.

But because some truths need silence more than words.)

Adhrika looked out of the window.

For the first time since she had begun telling her story...

she wasn't looking at the past.

She was simply...

looking.

After a long while...

she spoke.

**Adhrika**: Professor...

do you think...

I'll ever stop loving him?

(Vinay smiled gently.)

**Professor Vinay**: I don't know.

(She looked surprised.)

**Professor Vinay**: And I don't think that's the question I'd ask.

She waited.

**Professor Vinay**: I'd ask...

"What will that love become?"

People often imagine there are only two possibilities.

Either...

you love forever exactly as you do today...

or...

you stop loving completely.

Life is usually kinder...

and more subtle...

than that.

Sometimes love changes its shape.

It becomes gratitude.

Sometimes...

it becomes a lesson.

Sometimes...

it becomes a quiet blessing from a distance.

Sometimes...

it simply becomes part of who you are...

without asking anything from the other person.

(Adhrika listened carefully.)

**Professor Vinay**: Memories don't always need to disappear.

Sometimes...

they simply stop demanding that yesterday return.

(A tear rolled down Adhrika's cheek.

This time...

she didn't wipe it away immediately.)

It wasn't the desperate tear of someone drowning.

It felt...

lighter.

Almost peaceful.

After a few moments...

she smiled faintly.

**Adhrika**: I don't think I understand all of that yet.

Vinay chuckled.

**Professor Vinay**: Neither do I.

Not completely.

Every human being keeps learning.

Even professors.

She laughed.

A genuine laugh.

Not loud.

Not long.

But real.

(Vinay smiled with her.)

**Professor Vinay**: May I give you a small assignment?

Adhrika raised an eyebrow.

**Adhrika**: Homework?

**Professor Vinay**: The easiest homework you'll ever receive.

She folded her arms playfully.

**Adhrika**: Psychology professors always say that.

Vinay laughed.

**Professor Vinay**: Fair criticism.

Then he became thoughtful.

**Professor Vinay**: For one week...

don't try to stop your thoughts.

Don't fight them.

Don't chase them either.

Whenever your mind says,

"I wasn't enough..."

don't argue with it.

Simply ask one question.

"Is this a fact...

or is this a story my hurting mind is telling?"

Nothing more.

Don't force an answer.

Just ask.

Every single time.

(Adhrika repeated the question softly.)

**Adhrika**: A fact...

or a story...

(Vinay nodded.)

**Professor Vinay**: Curiosity often succeeds where force fails.

(The words settled gently into the room.)

A few moments later...

there was a knock.

Rudhika slowly opened the door.

She peeked inside.

**Rudhika**: Am I interrupting?

Vinay smiled.

**Professor Vinay**: Not at all.

Come in.

(Rudhika entered quietly.

Her eyes immediately searched her sister's face.)

She noticed the tears.

Her heart sank for a brief moment.

Then...

she noticed something else.

Her sister's shoulders were no longer as tense.

The lines on her forehead had softened.

And...

there it was.

A small smile.

Barely visible.

But unmistakably there.

(Rudhika's own eyes became moist.)

She walked over without saying anything.

Adhrika looked at her...

and smiled again.

This one was even smaller.

But it reached her eyes.

Rudhika couldn't stop herself.

She wrapped her sister in a gentle hug.

Adhrika hesitated...

then hugged her back.

Not out of obligation.

Out of affection.

For several seconds...

neither of them spoke.

(Vinay quietly looked away.

Some moments belong entirely to the people living them.)

When they finally separated...

Rudhika turned toward him.

**Rudhika**: Thank you, Professor.

Vinay shook his head.

**Professor Vinay**: Don't thank me.

Your sister did the difficult part today.

She chose honesty.

That takes courage.

(Adhrika looked down shyly.)

Vinay stood and walked them toward the door.

As they reached it...

he spoke one last time.

**Professor Vinay**: Adhrika.

She turned.

**Professor Vinay**: Don't measure today's success by whether you stopped hurting.

Measure it by something much smaller.

You walked into this room carrying your pain alone.

You're walking out having shared it.

Sometimes...

that is the first real step toward healing.

(She stood still for a moment.

Then she nodded.)

**Adhrika**: Thank you...

Professor.

Not because you fixed me.

But...

because you didn't try to.

You just...

stayed.

(Vinay smiled warmly.)

**Professor Vinay**: Human beings are not broken machines.

They don't always need fixing.

Sometimes...

they need someone willing to sit beside them while they find their own way.

(Adhrika carried those words with her.)

The two sisters stepped into the corridor.

The evening sun poured through the tall windows.

Students passed by without noticing them.

Rudhika glanced sideways.

**Rudhika**: Tea?

(Adhrika looked at her.)

For months...

her answer would have been,

"I'm not in the mood."

Today...

after thinking for a moment...

she answered differently.

**Adhrika**: Yes.

Tea sounds nice.

(Rudhika grinned.)

The two sisters walked slowly toward the college gate.

Not because all the pain had vanished.

It hadn't.

Not because every question had been answered.

They hadn't.

But because...

for the first time in a long time...

the future no longer looked like a closed door.

Behind them...

Professor Vinay stood by the office window.

He watched them leave.

He smiled.

Then he quietly returned to his desk.

The chessboard still lay there.

He opened it again.

The pieces were scattered from the afternoon's games.

One by one...

he placed each piece back in its proper place.

The king.

The queen.

The bishops.

The knights.

The rooks.

The pawns.

He looked at the completed board for a moment.

Then he softly whispered to the empty room.

**Professor Vinay**: Healing is a little like chess.

You cannot undo the moves already played.

But...

you can always choose your next move.

(He closed the chessboard gently.

Outside...

the sun dipped below the horizon.

Inside...

a new chapter had quietly begun.)

**End of Chapter 7**
